# PART ONE: HOW TO USE SUNO FOR HIMANT

## Understanding Suno — Two Modes

Suno AI has two ways to create music. You MUST know which mode to use for each scene:

---

### MODE 1 — INSTRUMENTAL / BACKGROUND SCORE (No Vocals)
For scenes where you need ambient sound, battle score, emotional underscore — NO singing.

**How to write the Suno prompt:**
- Use `[Instrumental]` as the FIRST tag
- Describe instruments, mood, tempo
- Write "no vocals, no lyrics" explicitly
- Add cinematic style tags

**Template:**
```
[Instrumental]
[No vocals, no lyrics, background score only]
[Genre: ...]
[Mood: ...]
[Instruments: ...]
[Tempo: ... BPM]
[Duration: ... seconds]
```

---

### MODE 2 — SONGS WITH LYRICS (Hindi Battle Chants, Emotional Songs)
For fight scenes, opening titles, emotional peaks — WITH vocals and lyrics.

**How to write the Suno prompt:**
- Write the ACTUAL LYRICS in the text box
- Use `[Verse]`, `[Chorus]`, `[Bridge]` tags to structure
- Add style description at the top
- Add `[Male vocals]` / `[Female vocals]` / `[Group chant]` as needed

**Template:**
```
Style: [genre, mood, instruments, vocal style]

[Intro]
(instrumental intro description)

[Verse 1]
actual lyrics here
each line on new line

[Chorus]
the hook/refrain here

[Outro]
```

---

### SUNO TIPS FOR HIMANT SPECIFICALLY

| What you want | How to get it in Suno |

# PART TWO: THE HIMANT SOUND IDENTITY — 5 CORE THEMES

Before any scene-specific prompts, these are the 5 SIGNATURE sounds of HIMANT.
Every other cue in the series derives from one of these.

---

## THEME 1 — THE TRISHUL MOTIF
*Used: Every time the Trishul appears. 3 notes. 3 instruments. Always.*

**What it sounds like:** Three instruments play the same three notes simultaneously but at different octaves — a bansuri flute (high), a low cello string (mid), and a deep temple bell strike (bass). The notes do not resolve — the third note hangs. It feels like an unanswered question.

**Suno Prompt:**
```
[Instrumental]
[No vocals, no lyrics]
[Style: Ancient Indian spiritual, cinematic leitmotif, sacred minimal]
[Mood: Sacred, haunting, unresolved, ancient covenant]
[Instruments: Bansuri flute high register, cello low string pluck, temple bell deep strike - three notes played in three-part harmony, sustained with long reverb]
[Tempo: Very slow, single phrase, 8 seconds total]
[Reverb: Large glacial cave reverb, long decay]
[Note: Three notes only - C, E, G - played once, overlapping, fading into silence]
[Loop-able, used as scene transition stinger]
```

---

## THEME 2 — HAIMAVAT THEME (The Ice People)
*Used: Settlement scenes, quiet moments, Arjak's emotional scenes*

**What it sounds like:** Sparse. Cold. Beautiful in the way that dangerous things are beautiful. A bansuri flute melody over a low drone string. Occasional wind texture. Feels ancient — like this music existed before human language.

**Suno Prompt:**
```
[Instrumental]
[No vocals, no lyrics, ambient background score]
[Style: Ancient Himalayan ambient, tribal folk, cinematic underscore, meditative]
[Mood: Cold beauty, ancient survival, quiet dignity, something precious and fragile]
[Instruments: Bansuri flute as primary melody (slow, breathy, folk scale), tanpura drone underneath, very sparse hand drum (one beat every 4 seconds), occasional wind texture layer, low string pad]
[Tempo: Very slow, 40 BPM, free-flowing]
[Key: D minor, pentatonic scale]
[Texture: Sparse — more silence than sound. The gaps between notes matter as much as the notes]
[Duration: 3 minutes, loop-able]
[Reverb: Large open mountain reverb, natural decay]
```

---

## THEME 3 — ASHVAGHOR THEME (The Death Riders)
*Used: Every scene in Rudraveg's camp, cavalry sequences, advancing army shots*

**What it sounds like:** Horse-rhythm drums. Low brass. Unstoppable forward momentum. Not evil — purposeful. The sound of something that has already decided.

**Suno Prompt:**
```
[Instrumental]
[No vocals, no lyrics, background score]
[Style: Epic tribal war march, ancient cavalry, cinematic action underscore]
[Mood: Inevitable, powerful, organized destruction, not chaotic but systematic]
[Instruments: Deep taiko-style war drums in horse-gallop rhythm (2-beat pattern), low brass drone (trombone/tuba equivalent), bone percussion rattles, distant battle horn calls, deep bass ostinato]
[Tempo: 80 BPM, strict march time, feels like hooves]
[Texture: Dense, heavy, the sound fills space completely — opposite of Haimavat theme]
[Duration: 3 minutes, loop-able]
[Build: Starts with drums only, brass enters at 30 seconds, full orchestra at 1 minute]
```

---

## THEME 4 — RUDRAVEG'S PERSONAL THEME
*Used: His quiet scenes, when he holds the miniature Trishul, the exile flashback*

**What it sounds like:** The Haimavat theme — but broken. Played in a minor key, slower, with a cello playing the bansuri melody instead of the flute. It is the Haimavat theme as heard by someone who was cast out of it. Grief as music.

**Suno Prompt:**
```
[Instrumental]
[No vocals, no lyrics]
[Style: Tragic cinematic underscore, broken folk theme, emotional restraint]
[Mood: Old grief, exile, something beautiful that was destroyed, dignity in loss]
[Instruments: Solo cello as primary (playing a folk melody that echoes the Haimavat theme but slower and lower), very low tanpura drone, single bansuri flute note held long in background, no percussion]
[Tempo: Very slow, 35 BPM]
[Key: D minor, same key as Haimavat theme but played in lower register]
[Note: This should sound like the Haimavat theme heard from very far away — recognizable but unreachable]
[Duration: 2 minutes]
[Dynamics: Pianissimo throughout — barely there, like a memory]
```

---

## THEME 5 — VRIDDHAK'S THEME (THE CALCULATION)
*Used: Vriddhak's scenes, the revelation episode, moments of manipulation*

**What it sounds like:** Deceptively calm. A slow, measured string quartet. Too orderly. Too controlled. The sound of a man who has made peace with terrible things. In later episodes, dissonant notes begin appearing in the melody — the cracks in the facade.

**Suno Prompt:**
```
[Instrumental]
[No vocals, no lyrics]
[Style: Chamber music, string quartet, deceptive calm, subtle dissonance]
[Mood: Controlled, calculating, the calm of someone who has long since decided, wisdom that hides cruelty]
[Instruments: String quartet (two violins, viola, cello), very measured and precise, classical structure with increasing dissonance]
[Tempo: 55 BPM, strict time — nothing improvised, nothing free]
[Texture: Each note placed deliberately — the opposite of organic. Feels composed rather than felt]
[Harmonic note: Begins consonant, introduces one dissonant note by the 30-second mark, returns to consonance — the dissonance appears and disappears as if it was never there]
[Duration: 90 seconds, loop-able]
```

### Scene — Bhedak Dies
*One arrow. Mid-laugh. Ordinary. Silence.*

**Sound Direction:** NO MUSIC. This is the most important sound cue in the series.

```
SOUND DESIGN NOTE FOR SUNO / EDITOR:
Do NOT use music here.

The audio for Bhedak's death is:
- His laugh (the last natural sound)
- The arrow (single sharp sound)
- Wind (continuous, indifferent)
- Snow falling (barely audible texture)
- Silence

After the DOLLY OUT shot of his body:
Wait 8 full seconds of complete silence.

Then — and only then — play the Trishul Motif (Theme 1) once.
Very quietly. Like the world acknowledging what happened.
Then silence again.

The absence of music IS the music.
The audience's discomfort with the silence IS the grief.
Do not rescue them from it with a score.
```
