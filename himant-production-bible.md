# PART ONE: FILM LANGUAGE — THE BEGINNER'S GLOSSARY
*(With HIMANT examples so every term means something real to you)*

---

## SHOTS — What the Camera Sees

### EXTREME WIDE SHOT (EWS) / ESTABLISHING SHOT
The camera is very far away. You see the entire world — mountains, sky, armies. The character is tiny or not visible at all.
**In HIMANT:** Opening shot — the endless snow-covered Himalayas at dawn. No people yet. Just the world.
**Why directors use it:** To say "HERE is where we are." It tells the audience: this place is vast, ancient, and indifferent to human pain.

### WIDE SHOT (WS)
You see the full character from head to toe, plus their environment.
**In HIMANT:** Arjak standing on a glacier ridge, axe in hand, army behind him stretching to the horizon.
**Why directors use it:** To show power, loneliness, scale. A character small against a large world = they are vulnerable. A character filling the frame = they own the space.

### MEDIUM SHOT (MS)
Waist up. The most common shot in dialogue scenes.
**In HIMANT:** Vriddhak speaking to Arjak in the war council. You see their faces and their hands.
**Why directors use it:** Hands tell stories. Vriddhak's burned Trishul palm is visible in medium shots — always.

### CLOSE-UP (CU)
Face only — from chin to forehead.
**In HIMANT:** Rudraveg's blue eyes when he hears Arjak's message. "Brother."
**Why directors use it:** Emotions live here. The audience enters the character's interior world.

### EXTREME CLOSE-UP (ECU)
One part of the face — only the eyes, only the lips, only a hand gripping a weapon.
**In HIMANT:** Arjak's fingers tightening around his axe handle. The Trishul etching on the haft fills the frame.
**Why directors use it:** It says: THIS DETAIL MATTERS. Pay attention. Remember this.

### INSERT SHOT
A cutaway to an object, a detail, a hand — outside the main action.
**In HIMANT:** Close on the diamond rune carved into Rudraveg's forehead — then cut back to the wide battle.
**Why directors use it:** Information and meaning dropped into the action without stopping it.

### POINT OF VIEW SHOT (POV)
The camera becomes the character's eyes. You see exactly what they see.
**In HIMANT:** Arjak's POV charging through a snowstorm battle — blurred warriors, torches, chaos. The audience is inside the fight.
**Why directors use it:** Total immersion. Fear becomes the audience's fear. Triumph becomes theirs.

### OVER THE SHOULDER SHOT (OTS)
Camera positioned behind one character's shoulder, looking at the other character.
**In HIMANT:** Every major conversation between Arjak and Rudraveg. You feel the distance — and the tension — between them.
**Why directors use it:** It captures both characters in one frame, showing their relationship through space.

### TWO-SHOT
Both characters visible in the same frame.
**In HIMANT:** Arjak and Bhedak riding side by side. Equal in frame = equal in the relationship.
**Why directors use it:** When the camera separates two characters (cutting between singles), there is distance between them. When it holds both in one frame, they are together.

### DUTCH ANGLE (CANTED SHOT)
The camera is tilted sideways. The horizon is not straight.
**In HIMANT:** Used only during Vriddhak's scenes from Episode 6 onward, when the audience begins to question him. The world itself is crooked around him.
**Why directors use it:** Unease. Something is wrong. Used sparingly so it MEANS something when it appears.

---

## CAMERA MOVEMENTS — How the Camera Moves

### PAN
Camera rotates left or right on a fixed axis. Like turning your head.
**In HIMANT:** Opening pan across the Ashvaghor army — thousands of warriors, torches, the camera slowly revealing the scale.
**Feel:** Discovery. Revelation. The slow pan builds dread.

### TILT
Camera rotates up or down on a fixed axis.
**In HIMANT:** Starting on Rudraveg's skull-decorated boots, tilting up slowly to his face — his full presence revealed from bottom to top.
**Feel:** Power. Intimidation. The character grows as the camera rises.

### DOLLY
Camera physically moves forward or backward on a track (or vehicle).
**DOLLY IN:** Camera moves toward the subject. The world closes in. Intimacy or threat.
**DOLLY OUT:** Camera moves away. The character gets smaller. Loss. Isolation.
**In HIMANT:** When Bhedak dies — DOLLY OUT slowly from his body. The world pulls away from him. He is already gone.

### TRACKING / FOLLOWING SHOT
Camera moves alongside a moving character.
**In HIMANT:** Tracking alongside Arjak and Bhedak as they run through a forest. The camera breathes with them.
**Feel:** Energy, urgency, companionship.

### CRANE / DRONE SHOT
Camera rises from ground level to high above — or descends from the sky.
**In HIMANT:** The final battle begins — crane shot rises from Arjak's face to reveal both armies stretching across the glacier. The scale of what has been built hits in one breath.
**Feel:** God's eye view. Fate arriving.

### STEADICAM / GIMBAL SHOT
Camera is worn on a stabilized rig, moving smoothly through space without cutting.
**In HIMANT:** The battle sequence in Episode 6 — one continuous shot weaving through warriors, never stopping, never cutting. The audience cannot rest.
**Feel:** You are IN the battle. No editing distance.

### HANDHELD
Camera is held by the operator — naturally shaky, organic, human.
**In HIMANT:** Used ONLY during the most emotionally raw scenes — Arjak learning Bhedak is dead. Virangi finding Dhrupad's message to Rudraveg.
**Feel:** Documentary truth. This moment is too real to be controlled.

### RACK FOCUS
Camera keeps the same position but shifts the focus from one subject to another.
**In HIMANT:** Arjak in focus (foreground) — then rack focus to Vriddhak watching him from the distance (background). What was blurry becomes sharp. A revelation.
**Feel:** One thing was true. Now another thing is truer.

## EDITING TERMS — How Scenes Are Put Together

### CUT
The most basic edit — one shot ends, the next begins.
**Hard Cut:** Immediate. No transition. Used for pace and shock.
**In HIMANT:** Bhedak laughing → CUT → Arjak being told he's dead. The hard cut IS the violence.

### MATCH CUT
Two shots that match in movement, shape, or action, cut together.
**In HIMANT:** Young Rudraveg carving the diamond rune into his forehead (flashback) → MATCH CUT to the same rune in Arjak's close-up as he touches it in the present. Past and present become one.
**Feel:** Connection across time. Memory made visible.

### CROSS-CUT / INTERCUTTING
Cutting between two simultaneous events happening in different locations.
**In HIMANT:** Episode 7 — cutting between Bhedak's patrol, Arjak's position, and Dhrupad's secret meeting. Three timelines happening at once. The audience knows what is coming before the characters do. This is called DRAMATIC IRONY — and it is devastating.

### JUMP CUT
A cut within the same scene that feels abrupt — time has jumped forward.
**In HIMANT:** Used in the exile flashback to show time passing in Rudraveg's solitary walk through the snow. He walks → jump cut → he is farther → jump cut → he is almost gone. Time accelerates. The exile has no end.

### MONTAGE
A series of short cuts edited together to compress time or build emotion.
**In HIMANT:** The training montage in Episode 3 — Arjak learning to fight from Vriddhak. But edited to show that Vriddhak is not teaching him to win. He is teaching him to survive long enough for the plan to work. In hindsight, every moment of "care" was calculation.

### L-CUT / J-CUT
The SOUND from the next scene starts before the picture changes (J-CUT) — or the picture changes but the SOUND from the previous scene continues (L-CUT).
**In HIMANT (L-CUT):** Rudraveg giving his army their orders (picture) — cut to the glacier landscape (picture changes) but his voice continues over the snow, fading. His words haunt the landscape.
**In HIMANT (J-CUT):** We hear the battle drums before we see the army. Sound arrives first. Dread precedes the image.

### DIRECTOR'S CUT vs EDITOR'S CUT
- **Editor's Cut:** The editor's first assembly of all footage. Raw. Long. Everything included.
- **Director's Cut:** The director works with the editor to shape the film to their vision.
- **Final Cut:** What the audience sees. In YouTube/independent production — the director usually has final cut.
**For HIMANT:** You as creator have final cut. Every choice belongs to you.

### SMASH CUT
A sudden, jarring cut — usually to silence after noise, or noise after silence.
**In HIMANT:** The height of the battle — steel, screaming, drums — SMASH CUT to absolute silence. Arjak alone in the snow. The war is over. The silence is louder than the battle was.

## DIRECTOR'S TERMS

### COVERAGE
Filming the same scene from multiple angles so the editor has choices.
Every dialogue scene should have: wide (both characters), over-the-shoulder (each direction), close-up (each character), insert shots.

### BLOCKING
Where the actors move within the frame. Deliberate. Every step means something.
**In HIMANT:** In the final confrontation, Arjak and Rudraveg begin 10 meters apart. As the conversation progresses, they slowly close the distance — without being told to, the audience feels the relationship changing through physical space.

### MOTIVATED CAMERA MOVE
The camera only moves when a character moves, or when an emotion demands it. The camera never moves for no reason.
**Rule for HIMANT:** If the camera moves, something is changing — inside the character or in the world.

### GOLDEN HOUR
The hour after sunrise or before sunset. The light is warm, orange, magical.
**In HIMANT:** Arjak and Bhedak's friendship scenes are ALL shot in golden hour — warmth that will be stolen. The audience learns: golden light = this will be taken from us.

### PRACTICAL LIGHT
Light sources that exist within the scene — torches, fire, moonlight on snow.
**In HIMANT:** Every battle scene is lit only by torches and fire. No artificial studio light. The flames flicker. Nothing is stable. This is deliberate.

# EPISODE 1: हार (HAAR — DEFEAT)
**Runtime: 8 minutes | Reference: Christopher Nolan**
**Emotional core: The ground falls away. Nothing is what it seemed.**

---

## SCENE 1 — THE WORLD BEFORE THE STORY
**Duration: 45 seconds**

**SHOT:** EXTREME WIDE SHOT — DRONE
The Himalayas at the edge of dawn. Not beautiful. Terrifying. The camera drifts above an endless white world — glaciers cracked like old bone, ridgelines sharp as blades, valleys still lost in darkness. The sky is the color of a bruise healing. Somewhere below, something is burning. The smoke rises thin and black against the white.

**SOUND:** Absolute silence for 3 seconds. Then — a single deep bass note. The Trishul motif: three instruments at three pitches, played simultaneously. It hums like the earth itself has a voice.

**DIRECTOR'S NOTE:** No title card yet. No character. No dialogue. The audience must feel they have been transported. They are not watching a film. They are waking up in a world that is older than anything they know. Hold this shot until the audience is uncomfortable. Then hold it three seconds more.

**EDITOR'S NOTE:** Do not rush out of this shot. The entire emotional contract of the series is established here — this world will not comfort you.

---

## SCENE 2 — THE END (THAT WE WILL SPEND 10 EPISODES UNDERSTANDING)
**Duration: 2 minutes 30 seconds**

**SHOT 1:** WIDE SHOT — STATIC
A glacier valley floor. Thousands of warriors stand in formation — the ASHVAGHOR army, stretching beyond what the eye can hold. Black flags bearing the Trishul symbol snap in the wind (the Haimavat's sacred symbol, captured and flown as trophy). Torches held by row after row of soldiers. Snow falls steadily. Nobody moves.

**SOUND:** War drums. Slow. One beat every two seconds. Like a heart that has decided to stop rushing.

**SHOT 2:** MEDIUM SHOT
In the center of this silence: ARJAK.
He is on his knees in the snow. Not collapsed — kneeling deliberately, as if he chose this position. His stone axe is on the ground before him. His hands are on his thighs. His back is straight. His face is — unreadable. Not defeated. Not afraid. Something else. Something the audience does not yet have a word for.

Behind him, in chains: the HAIMAVAT people. Warriors, elders, children. Not dead. Captured.

**SHOT 3:** CLOSE-UP on the TRISHUL-SHIELD
It rests against a stone. It has been placed, not dropped. Someone treated it with care even after taking it. The silver Trishul gleams in torchlight.

**DIRECTOR'S NOTE:** The audience must not know if this is victory or defeat for Arjak. The kneeling reads as surrender — but something in his posture says otherwise. Cast and direct this specifically: Arjak is not broken. He is waiting.

**SHOT 4:** WIDE SHOT
RUDRAVEG walks through his own army. The soldiers part for him. He wears no helmet. He is not performing — he walks the way a man walks to something he has been moving toward his entire life.

He reaches Arjak.

He stops.

He looks down at the kneeling man.

For a long moment: nothing.

**SHOT 5:** EXTREME CLOSE-UP — RUDRAVEG'S EYES
Blue. The same shade as Arjak's. The audience does not know this yet. But the camera holds on the eyes long enough that something registers — a flicker of recognition that they cannot name.

**SHOT 6:** OVER THE SHOULDER — Rudraveg looking down at Arjak
Rudraveg slowly leans forward. He says something.

It is a WHISPER. The drums stop. The wind stops — or seems to. The words are:

**RUDRAVEG** *(barely audible, in old Haimavat tongue — NO SUBTITLES)*
*"Vaa. Ab ghar chaltey hain."*

He straightens up. His expression: not triumph. Grief that has finished.

**SHOT 7:** CLOSE-UP — ARJAK
He heard the words. His expression shifts — not relief, not surrender, not victory. Something that lives between all three. The ghost of something that might, in a different world, be called understanding.

**SMASH CUT TO BLACK.**

**White text on black:**
*हिमंत*

*9 महीने पहले*
*(9 months earlier)*

**DIRECTOR'S NOTE:** The whisper is NOT subtitled. The audience is denied the information. This single choice — withholding four words — drives every episode that follows. What did he say? Why did Arjak's face change? The answer comes in Episode 10. And when it does, it recontextualizes this entire scene.

**EDITOR'S NOTE:** Hold on the black after the title card. Three full seconds of black and silence. Let the audience sit in not-knowing. Then —

---

## SCENE 3 — THE WORLD AS IT WAS (9 MONTHS AGO)
**Duration: 45 seconds**

**SHOT:** MATCH CUT from black to —
An extreme wide shot of the SAME VALLEY — but 9 months earlier. The glacier is fuller. The sky is lighter. And there is sound — the living sound of a settlement. Dogs. Children. The rhythm of daily life.

**SHOT:** TRACKING SHOT — following a young girl running between animal-hide tents, past warriors sharpening weapons, past women weaving from mammoth wool, past a fire where elders sit in early morning. Life.

**DIRECTOR'S NOTE:** This is what we will spend 10 episodes losing. Show it abundantly. Let the audience love this world so that its destruction — which they have already seen — becomes unbearable.

---

## SCENE 4 — ARJAK RETURNS FROM THE HUNT
**Duration: 1 minute**

**SHOT 1:** WIDE SHOT — the settlement's upper entrance
ARJAK and four warriors emerge from the snow-pass, carrying the carcass of a woolly deer between them. They are laughing about something — the aftermath of a joke we didn't hear. BHEDAK is mid-punchline. The other warriors groan.

**SHOT 2:** MEDIUM SHOT — BHEDAK
BHEDAK (भेदक) — broad, alive in a way that takes up more space than his body should. Dreadlocks wrapped in a loose knot. Eyes that are always slightly amused, even now, even after a cold hunt. He finishes his joke. One warrior laughs. The rest walk away, which Bhedak takes as the highest compliment.

**BHEDAK** *(calling after the departing warriors)*
*"Tumhara hasna aur rona ek hi cheez hai. Dono mujhe pata hi nahi chalte."*
("Your laughing and your crying are the same thing. I can never tell the difference.")

**SHOT 3:** CLOSE-UP — ARJAK
Not quite laughing. A smile that knows it is allowed to exist right now and is rationing it. He scans the settlement as he enters — not with suspicion, but with the habitual awareness of someone who has never fully stopped being a watchman.

His eyes catch something: VRIDDHAK, on the far side of the settlement, watching the returning hunters. Not smiling.

**SHOT 4:** RACK FOCUS
Arjak in focus (foreground, smiling) — rack to Vriddhak in background (watching, calculating). The audience sees the shift before Arjak does.

**SHOT 5:** ARJAK'S POV
Vriddhak holds his gaze for a moment, then turns and disappears into his dwelling. Arjak watches the space where he was.

**BHEDAK** *(beside him, having noticed)*
*"Woh hamesha aise hi dekhta hai. Jaise tum sirf uski yaadasht ho."*
("He always looks at you like that. Like you are just a memory of his.")

ARJAK says nothing. He sets down his end of the carcass.

**DIRECTOR'S NOTE:** This exchange between Bhedak and Arjak should feel unremarkable — two friends noticing something odd and moving past it. The line "just a memory of his" should not land as significant in Episode 1. When the audience returns to it after Episode 8, it will shatter them. Plant seeds invisibly.

---

## SCENE 5 — THE CELEBRATION (AND THE WATCHER)
**Duration: 1 minute 15 seconds**

**SHOT 1:** WIDE SHOT — the settlement fire circle at night
The tribe celebrates the successful hunt. Food, fire, the percussion of bone-drums and stretched hide. Children chase each other through the light and shadow. Warriors drink fermented mountain barley.

**SHOT 2:** TRACKING SHOT — moving through the celebration
The camera moves through the firelight like a ghost, observing. A warrior arm-wrestling. Two elders in quiet argument. A young girl placing a small carved Trishul into the fire as offering — the flames eat it, and the fire briefly burns silver. (INSERT: a single frame of the flame color. The audience will notice on second viewing.)

**SHOT 3:** MEDIUM SHOT — VIRANGI
She sits at the edge of the firelight, not in it. Weapon being cleaned methodically. She watches the celebration with an expression that is not cold and not warm — it is the face of someone who loves something they don't quite trust. Her obsidian blades catch the firelight.

**ARJAK (V.O.)** *(sitting down beside her)*
*"Tu kabhi sirf baithti kyun nahi?"*
("Why do you never just sit?")

**VIRANGI** *(without looking up from the blade)*
*"Main baithti hoon. Tum dekhte nahi ho."*
("I am sitting. You are not looking.")

A beat. Arjak looks at her.

**ARJAK**
*"Tujhe kuch pata hai jo mujhe nahi pata?"*
("Do you know something I don't?")

**VIRANGI** *(now she looks at him. Directly. A half-second too long.)*
*"Haan."*
("Yes.")

She goes back to the blade. Arjak stares.

**ARJAK**
*"Kya?"*
("What?")

**VIRANGI**
*"Bahut kuch."*
("Many things.")

She stands and walks away into the dark. Arjak watches her go. He is not sure if he was just warned or just teased.

**DIRECTOR'S NOTE:** Virangi must not be likeable in the easy way. She is challenging. The audience should not know what to make of her in Episode 1. By Episode 5, they will love her absolutely. That journey requires that she not be immediately warm.

**SHOT 4:** WIDE SHOT
The celebration continues. Arjak alone at the edge of it. The fire. The people. His.

He does not know — yet — that this is the last peaceful night any of them will have.

---

## SCENE 6 — VRIDDHAK'S WARNING
**Duration: 1 minute 30 seconds**

**SHOT 1:** INT. VRIDDHAK'S DWELLING — NIGHT
Stone walls. Animal furs. In the center: a low fire, and on a stone altar, the TRISHUL-SHIELD. Not the one we saw captured in Episode 1's future — this is earlier, before. It sits in its rightful place, the silver gleaming in firelight. SACRED.

**SHOT 2:** MEDIUM SHOT — VRIDDHAK
He stands with his back to the entrance as ARJAK enters. He does not turn.

**VRIDDHAK**
*"Aaj ki raat ki raid — woh raid nahi thi."*
("Tonight's raid we repelled — it was not a raid.")

Arjak stops.

**ARJAK**
*"Toh kya thi?"*
("Then what was it?")

**SHOT 3:** CLOSE-UP — VRIDDHAK'S FACE as he finally turns
His expression is the most frightening thing in the episode so far — calm. Not the calm of safety. The calm of a man who already knows what is coming.

**VRIDDHAK**
*"Ginti. Woh hum ko ginn rahe the. Kitne hain. Kahan hain. Kitne bachche. Kitne lad saktey hain."*
("A census. They were counting us. How many. Where we are. How many children. How many can fight.")

Silence.

**ARJAK**
*"Kitni baar?"*
("How many raids? How long have they been counting?")

**VRIDDHAK** *(a pause that is exactly one second too long)*
*"Kafi."*
("Enough.")

**SHOT 4:** INSERT — Vriddhak's hand on the Trishul-shield
The burn-scar on his palm — a Trishul scar, self-inflicted, the mark of the Keeper. He touches the shield without looking at it. Like a man touching something he owns, not something he worships.

**ARJAK**
*"Hum kya karein?"*
("What do we do?")

**VRIDDHAK**
*"Jo hamesha karna tha. Apne log ko bachao. Trishul ko bachao."*
("What was always meant to be done. Save our people. Save the Trishul.")

He removes his hand from the shield.

**VRIDDHAK** *(quietly, almost to himself)*
*"Par pehle — tujhe kuch seekhna hoga."*
("But first — you must learn something.")

**DIRECTOR'S NOTE:** Every scene between Vriddhak and Arjak must be shot with Vriddhak slightly elevated — standing while Arjak sits, on a step above him, framed higher in the shot. Physical hierarchy that the audience absorbs without realizing. When Arjak finally stands LEVEL with Vriddhak in Episode 9, the audience will feel the shift in their body before they understand it intellectually.

**SHOT 5:** CLOSE-UP — ARJAK
He nods. He trusts this man completely.

The audience, seeing this moment after Episode 8, will find it unbearable.

---

## SCENE 7 — EPISODE 1 CLOSING: THE HORIZON
**Duration: 30 seconds**

**SHOT 1:** EXT. SETTLEMENT WALLS — NIGHT
ARJAK stands at the highest point of the settlement wall, looking south. The celebration sounds behind him, distant.

**SHOT 2:** ARJAK'S POV
The valley below. Darkness. Somewhere in that darkness — a distant orange glow. Far away. Barely visible. But there.

Fires. Ashvaghor fires. Moving.

**SHOT 3:** EXTREME CLOSE-UP — ARJAK'S EYES
He watches. He does not yet know what he is looking at. He only knows that it is too many fires for anything good.

**SHOT 4:** WIDE SHOT — PULLING BACK slowly via DOLLY OUT
Arjak getting smaller. The settlement behind him. The fires in the valley below. The mountains above. The sky.

**SOUND:** The Trishul motif again. Three notes. But this time: slower. And the third note does not resolve. It hangs.

**SMASH CUT TO BLACK.**

**EPISODE TITLE CARD:**
*हार*
*(Defeat)*

**DIRECTOR'S NOTE:** The episode title appears at the END, not the beginning. The audience now understands why it is called "Defeat" — but they don't know yet if the defeat they saw at the start is the kind of defeat they think it is.

# EPISODE 2: जड़ (JAD — ROOTS)
**Runtime: 7 minutes | Reference: Baahubali**
**Emotional core: This world is worth fighting for. And someone is already inside it.**

---

## SCENE 1 — THE SCOUTING PARTY
**Duration: 1 minute 30 seconds**

**SHOT 1:** STEADICAM — moving fast through dense snow-forest
ARJAK leads a scouting party of six through the middle altitude forest — the borderland between Haimavat glacier territory and the warmer valleys below. Everything here is transitional: half-frozen streams, trees that are both alive and ice-covered, light that cannot decide between shadow and silver.

**SHOT 2:** TRACKING SHOT alongside Arjak
He moves without sound. Communicating with his warriors through hand signals — the Haimavat sign language of the hunt, now applied to war. Three fingers: split. Flat hand: stop. Circle: regroup.

**SHOT 3:** ARJAK STOPS
His hand shoots up: STOP.

Everyone freezes.

**SHOT 4:** ARJAK'S POV
Through the tree-line: a column of ASHVAGHOR soldiers. Not raiders — soldiers. Organized. In formation. Moving with discipline. Carrying not raiding supplies but SURVEYING equipment — long poles, knotted ropes for measurement.

And there are horses. This alone changes everything.

**SHOT 5:** CLOSE-UP — ARJAK'S FACE
He is doing math. His expression: fear, controlled immediately into calculation.

**BHEDAK** *(barely a breath beside him)*
*"Yeh raider nahi hain."*
("These are not raiders.")

**ARJAK** *(same breath-volume)*
*"Nahi."*
("No.")

**SHOT 6:** INSERT — the Ashvaghor formation
At the rear of the column: an officer watching the measurement work. He makes a mark on a stretched hide map. He is recording the territory.

**ARJAK** *(to his unit, barely audible)*
*"Wapas. Abhi."*
("Back. Now.")

---

## SCENE 2 — THE TRISHUL REVEALED (LORE SCENE)
**Duration: 1 minute 45 seconds**

**SHOT 1:** INT. ICE-TEMPLE — DAY
The Haimavat's sacred space carved into the glacier itself. Walls of ancient ice, blue-white, translucent. Torches set in iron brackets. And at the center: a raised stone platform where the TRISHUL-SHIELD rests, not as decoration but as the centerpiece of a living practice.

WAR-PRIESTS in white ash paint perform a slow ritual around it. Their movements are deliberate and silent.

**SHOT 2:** WIDE SHOT — ARJAK and VRIDDHAK enter together
Arjak has never been inside the full ceremonial chamber before. He stops. The scale of it — the ancient cold, the blue ice, the ritual — lands on him physically.

**VRIDDHAK** *(quietly, pride and possession mixed)*
*"Trishul sirf hamar nishan nahi hai. Yeh hamaari zameen ka dil hai."*
("The Trishul is not merely our symbol. It is the heart of our land.")

**SHOT 3:** SLOW PUSH IN toward the Trishul-shield
The silver catches torchlight. The three prongs cast three shadows on the ice wall — each shadow pointing in a different direction.

**VRIDDHAK (V.O.)**
*"Teen dhaarein — teeno nadi ke srot. Jab yeh barf pighlegi, teen nadi janmeingi. Jiske paas Trishul hai, woh yeh samjhega ki nadi kahaan se nikalti hain."*
("Three prongs — three river sources. When this ice melts, three rivers will be born. Whoever holds the Trishul will understand where the rivers emerge.")

**SHOT 4:** CLOSE-UP — ARJAK
Processing this. He believed the Trishul was spiritual. A symbol of faith. He is hearing for the first time that it may be something more practical. He files this away.

**ARJAK**
*"Aur agar Ashvaghor ko pata chal gaya?"*
("And if the Ashvaghor find out?")

**VRIDDHAK** *(beat — one second too long)*
*"Isliye hum ladte hain."*
("That is why we fight.")

**DIRECTOR'S NOTE:** Vriddhak's pause before his answer — one second. Barely perceptible. But the audience, on second viewing, will notice. He was deciding how much truth to tell. He chose the minimum.

## SCENE 3 — THE DYING SCOUT
**Duration: 45 seconds**

**SHOT 1:** EXT. SETTLEMENT BORDER — LATE AFTERNOON
Commotion at the border gate. Warriors carrying something — a body, still alive but barely.

**SHOT 2:** CLOSE-UP — THE ASHVAGHOR SCOUT
Young. Injured beyond recovery. An Ashvaghor warrior, captured or wounded before he could retreat. The diamond rune is marked on his pauldron — he is Rudraveg's direct command unit.

**SHOT 3:** MEDIUM SHOT — Arjak kneeling beside him
He asks in the Ashvaghor dialect — he knows enough:

**ARJAK**
*"Kaun bheja tumhe? Tumhara sardaar kaun hai?"*
("Who sent you? Who is your commander?")

The scout's eyes are going. But his lips move.

**SCOUT** *(barely, in Ashvaghor dialect — SUBTITLED)*
*"The Warlord asks... is the boy still alive?"*

He dies.

**SHOT 4:** CLOSE-UP — ARJAK

**SHOT 4:** CLOSE-UP — ARJAK'S FACE
He is thinking about the dying man's question. "What are you to him?"

**VIRANGI** *(beside him, appearing without announcement)*
*"Woh phir aayenge."*
("They will come again.")

**ARJAK**
*"Haan."*

**VIRANGI**
*"Iss baar unhone sab kuch nahi lagaya. Woh test kar rahe the."*
("This time they didn't bring everything. They were testing.")

**ARJAK** *(turning to her)*
*"Kya test?"*
("Testing what?")

**VIRANGI** *(a beat)*
*"Hum kitni der tak hold kar sakte hain."*
("How long we can hold.")

# EPISODE 7: गद्दारी (GADDARI — BETRAYAL)
**Runtime: 10 minutes | Reference: GOT**
**Emotional core: There is no armor against this. No amount of skill saves you from what ordinary betrayal can do.**

---

## THE STRUCTURE OF THIS EPISODE
This episode is told in THREE SIMULTANEOUS TIMELINES of the same day — pre-dawn to midday. The editor cuts between them, building toward the convergence.

**TIMELINE A:** Bhedak's patrol
**TIMELINE B:** Arjak's position (unaware)
**TIMELINE C:** Dhrupad's pre-dawn meeting

The audience sees all three. The dramatic irony — we know what is happening; the characters do not — is the most painful tool in the filmmaker's kit. Use it here in full.

---

## TIMELINE C (PRE-DAWN) — DHRUPAD'S MEETING
**Duration: 1 minute 30 seconds — shown in fragments throughout the episode**

**SHOT 1:** EXT. FOREST — 3 AM
DHRUPAD, alone. Waiting.

**SHOT 2:** AN ASHVAGHOR MESSENGER emerges from the dark
They do not greet each other. No ceremony. Two people who hate this transaction but cannot stop it.

**SHOT 3:** CLOSE-UP — DHRUPAD'S FACE
He is not a man at ease with what he is doing. His hands: they shake slightly. He controls them. Every line of his face is a person trying to be two people at once.

He hands the messenger a rolled piece of hide.

**SHOT 4:** INSERT — the hide, unrolled
Patrol route. Bhedak's route. Timing. Position. Everything.

**SHOT 5:** CLOSE-UP — DHRUPAD after the messenger leaves
He stands alone in the dark forest.

He does not look like a traitor. He looks like a father.

He takes from his clothing a small carved figure — a child's toy, Haimavat-made. He holds it. His daughter made this. She is three years gone.

He puts it away and walks back to the settlement.

**DIRECTOR'S NOTE:** Do NOT show this scene in linear order. Fragment it across the episode — small pieces between Bhedak's patrol and Arjak's scenes. Each fragment of Timeline C adds context that makes what happens in Timeline A worse. The audience assembles the truth in real time.

---

## TIMELINE A — BHEDAK'S PATROL
**Duration: 3 minutes — in fragments**

**SHOT 1:** DAWN. BHEDAK leaves the settlement.
He has scouted this route a hundred times. His body knows it. He moves easily, confidently, making small sounds: clicking his tongue at intervals. His habit. His rhythm.

**SHOT 2:** THE ROUTE
Familiar terrain. A narrow pass, then open snow, then the forest ridge. He has done this in his sleep.

**SHOT 3:** BHEDAK notices something
On the rock face: old horse tracks. Ashvaghor cavalry, but old — days old. He crouches. Studies them. Decides: old enough to be residual from the last engagement. He marks the position mentally.

He decides to continue forward.

**SHOT 4:** BHEDAK, turning back
He reaches the end of the patrol. No threats. He begins the return route.

He is, at this moment, ninety seconds from the safety of the settlement's sight range.

**SHOT 5:** BHEDAK has a thought
He is almost back. He smiles. He makes a clicking sound with his tongue.

*"Arjak ko bataunga — woh akela bhi nahi reh sakta ghante bhar."*
("I'll tell Arjak — he can't even be alone for an hour.")

He is laughing at a joke he is making to himself.

**SHOT 6:** THE SHOT
It comes from the forest ridge to the right. One arrow. Not several — one. The Ashvaghor sniper was told exactly where to stand.

Bhedak stops.

He looks down.

**SHOT 7:** EXTREME CLOSE-UP — HIS FACE
He is mid-laugh. His expression has not yet caught up to what has happened to his body.

He looks confused — the confusion of a man whose body is telling him something his mind hasn't accepted yet.

He sits down in the snow. Carefully. The way you sit when you are very tired.

His last sound is the end of the laugh that was already leaving his throat when the arrow arrived.

It is quiet.

The snow falls.

Nobody is there but him.

**SHOT 8:** WIDE SHOT — PULLING BACK — DOLLY OUT
Bhedak in the snow. Getting smaller. The forest closing in around him. The world pulling back from him.

He is alone. He was always alone, in the end, in this moment that was written when Dhrupad handed over the hide three hours ago.

**SOUND:** Silence. Absolute. The Trishul motif — played once, very quietly, then stopping.

**DIRECTOR'S NOTE:** No music. No drums. No swelling orchestral grief. This death does not get a score. The silence is the score. Give the audience nothing to hold onto but the emptiness of the shot.

---

## TIMELINE B — ARJAK IS TOLD
**Duration: 1 minute**

**SHOT 1:** INT. SETTLEMENT — MIDDAY
A warrior appears at the door. His face: the face of someone who has been made to carry something they don't want to be carrying.

**SHOT 2:** ARJAK looks up from the map table
He reads the warrior's face before the warrior speaks.

**SHOT 3:** CLOSE-UP — ARJAK
He very carefully puts down the charcoal he was marking with. He does this because if he doesn't do something deliberate with his hands right now they will not be under his control.

**SHOT 4:** WIDE SHOT
He walks out of the hall. Not running. Walking. Very carefully.

---

## THE ARROW
**Duration: 45 seconds**

**SHOT 1:** EXT. SNOWFIELD — AFTERNOON
Arjak crouches over the arrow that was pulled from Bhedak's body. He holds it in his hands.

**SHOT 2:** INSERT — the arrow
Ashvaghor-made. But the fletching color: specific. Not front-line troops. Not raiders. A specific command unit that operates under Rudraveg's direct authority — a unit that would only be deployed to a precise location if given exact coordinates.

**SHOT 3:** CLOSE-UP — ARJAK'S HANDS
They have stopped shaking. That is the frightening thing — they were shaking a moment ago. Now they are still.

**SHOT 4:** ARJAK stands. Turns.

**SHOT 5:** WIDE SHOT
DHRUPAD has come to help with the body preparation. He approaches Arjak.

They are three feet apart.

**SHOT 6:** THE MOMENT
Neither speaks.

Arjak looks at Dhrupad.

The arrow in Arjak's hand.

Dhrupad's face — appropriate grief, appropriate concern. But something else. The eyes are calculating, not grieving.

Arjak looks at the arrow. Looks at Dhrupad.

He does not accuse. He does not act.

He looks away.

**SHOT 7:** CLOSE-UP — DHRUPAD
He releases a breath he was holding.

**SHOT 8:** CLOSE-UP — ARJAK
His jaw. His eyes. He is not a man who doesn't know. He is a man who knows and is deciding — very carefully, in a grief that has found a cold place to live — what to do with knowing.

**SMASH CUT TO BLACK.**

# EPISODE 8: सत्य (SATYA — TRUTH)
**Runtime: 10 minutes | Reference: Baahubali + Nolan — THE REVELATION**
**Emotional core: The floor was glass the whole time. Now it breaks.**

---

## THE CONFRONTATION WITH DHRUPAD
**Duration: 2 minutes**

**SHOT 1:** INT. STORAGE CHAMBER — NIGHT
Private. Stone walls. One torch. ARJAK has asked DHRUPAD to come alone. DHRUPAD knows why.

**SHOT 2:** TWO-SHOT — they face each other
For a moment: just silence.

**ARJAK**
*"Bhed — bata."*
("Tell me.")

**DHRUPAD** *(a beat, then)*
*"Kya chahta hai tu sunna?"*
("What do you want to hear?")

**ARJAK**
*"Sach."*
("Truth.")

The longest pause.

**DHRUPAD** *(quietly, the act of saying it is the release of something held for years)*
*"Haaan."*
("Yes.")

**SHOT 3:** CLOSE-UP — ARJAK
He closes his eyes for exactly one second. That is all he allows himself.

Then:

**ARJAK**
*"Kyun?"*
("Why?")

**SHOT 4:** DHRUPAD
He does not justify. He explains.

**DHRUPAD**
*"Meri beti. Teen saal pehle. Ashvaghor ne usse le liya. Woh zinda hai. Rudraveg ke camp mein. Woh jaanta hai main woh jaanta hoon. Aur woh jaanta hai main kuch bhi kar sakta hoon usse bachane ke liye."*
("My daughter. Three years ago. The Ashvaghor took her. She is alive. In Rudraveg's camp. He knows I know this. And he knows I will do anything to save her.")

**SHOT 5:** CLOSE-UP — ARJAK
The rage he expected to feel is not there. What is there is worse: understanding. He understands completely. That is the worst thing about this betrayal — there is no monster. There is only a father.

**ARJAK** *(after a long silence)*
*"Bhedak..."*

He cannot finish the sentence.

**DHRUPAD** *(his voice breaking for the first and only time)*
*"Main janta tha. Aur phir bhi kiya."*
("I knew. And I did it anyway.")

---

## THE VRIDDHAK REVELATION — THE KATTAPPA MOMENT
**Duration: 4 minutes — THE SERIES' CENTERPIECE**

**SHOT 1:** INT. VRIDDHAK'S DWELLING — LATER
ARJAK enters without ceremony. He is past ceremony.

**VRIDDHAK** *(without turning)*
*"Main jaanta tha tu aayega."*
("I knew you would come.")

**ARJAK**
*"Dhrupad ne bataya."*
("Dhrupad told me.")

**VRIDDHAK**
*"Haan."*

**ARJAK**
*"Tum jaante the."*
("You knew.")

A pause.

**VRIDDHAK** *(turning now)*
*"Haan. Main jaanta tha."*
("Yes. I knew.")

**ARJAK** *(barely controlled)*
*"Bhedak mar gaya. Tum jaante the. Aur kuch nahi kiya."*
("Bhedak is dead. You knew. And you did nothing.")

**SHOT 2:** CLOSE-UP — VRIDDHAK
His expression: not guilt. Something worse — the expression of a man who has long since made peace with what he is.

**VRIDDHAK**
*"Baith jaa, Arjak. Ab waqt hai ki tujhe sab kuch pata chale."*
("Sit down, Arjak. It is time for you to know everything.")

**SHOT 3:** THE REVELATION BEGINS
Vriddhak moves to the stone altar. He takes the Trishul-shield in both hands — the burn scar on his palm touching it.

**VRIDDHAK**
*"Rudraveg — woh Haimavat ka dushman nahi tha. Woh Haimavat ka aadmi tha. Mera pota."*
("Rudraveg — he was not the Haimavat's enemy. He was a Haimavat man. My grandson.")

Silence.

**SHOT 4:** CLOSE-UP — ARJAK
He does not respond. He waits.

**VRIDDHAK**
*"Meri ek beti thi. Usne ek Ashvaghor aadmi se pyaar kiya. Mujhe yeh pasand nahi aaya. Par woh aazad thi. Usne do bete diye aur marr gayi."*
("I had a daughter. She loved an Ashvaghor man. I did not approve. But she was free. She gave two sons and died.")

**SHOT 5:** MEDIUM SHOT — VRIDDHAK
*"Bada beta — Rudraveg — main use chhupa nahi sakta tha. Toh usne Haimavat ke ek abandoned child ki tarah rakha. Kisi ko pata nahi tha."*
("The elder son — Rudraveg — I could not keep him hidden. So I placed him as an abandoned child among the Haimavat. No one knew.")

*"Chhota beta — Mahashil — woh tera baap tha. Use main apne paas rakha. Apna successor banana chahta tha."*
("The younger son — Mahashil — he was your father. I kept him with me. I wanted to make him my successor.")

**SHOT 6:** ARJAK — STANDING STILL
He is doing the mathematics. The terrible mathematics.

*Mahashil is his father.*
*Mahashil and Rudraveg are brothers.*
*Rudraveg is —*

**ARJAK** *(barely voiced)*
*"Rudraveg... mere chacha hai."*
("Rudraveg... is my uncle.")

**VRIDDHAK**
*"Haan."*

**SHOT 7:** WIDE SHOT
The room is enormous in this moment. The two men — the space between them — the weight of thirty years of lies in the air between them.

**ARJAK**
*"Aur tune... usse nikaala kyun?"*
("And you... exiled him. Why?")

**VRIDDHAK**
*"Kyunki usne wahi padha jo Trishul ke baare mein hai. Jo sirf main jaanta tha. Agar woh jaanta hai toh woh Trishul ko lega. Jo mujhe khatam kar deta."*
("Because he read what exists about the Trishul. What only I knew. If he knows, he will take the Trishul. Which ends me.")

**ARJAK**
*"Toh tune use banaya. Dushman. Yeh poori jung — yeh teri wajah se hai."*
("So you made him. The enemy. This entire war — it is because of you.")

**VRIDDHAK** *(calmly)*
*"Is war ki wajah se duniya nahi badlegi. Duniya tabhi badlegi jab dono khoon mil jayenge — tere aur Rudraveg ke — aur Trishul ko uthayein saath. Prophesy."*
("The war alone will not change the world. The world will change when both bloods unite — yours and Rudraveg's — and lift the Trishul together. Prophecy.")

**SHOT 8:** CLOSE-UP — ARJAK
He understands now. Vriddhak has been engineering this encounter for thirty years. Not as cruelty. As calculation. He genuinely believes this ends the war and saves the civilization. He has been playing both sides like instruments.

**ARJAK**
*"Aur Dhrupad? Use jaante hue bhi chhodh diya?"*
("And Dhrupad? You knew about him too and allowed it?")

**VRIDDHAK**
*"Dhrupad ka information Rudraveg ko yahan laata. Tere paas laata. Yahi chahiye tha."*
("Dhrupad's information would bring Rudraveg here. To you. That is what was needed.")

A long silence.

**ARJAK** *(quietly, with a devastation that is ice-cold)*
*"Bhedak mar gaya kyunki TU chahta tha."*
("Bhedak died because YOU wanted it.")

**VRIDDHAK** *(not flinching)*
*"Haan."*

**SHOT 9:** ARJAK TURNS
He walks to the door. His back to Vriddhak.

**ARJAK** *(at the door, not turning)*
*"Rudraveg ko ek sandesh bhej. Ek shabd — purani Haimavat boli mein."*
("Send Rudraveg a message. One word — in the old Haimavat tongue.")

**VRIDDHAK**
*"Kaunsa shabd?"*
("Which word?")

**ARJAK**
*"Bhai."*
("Brother.")

He leaves.

**SHOT 10:** CLOSE-UP — VRIDDHAK
Alone. The Trishul-shield in his hands.

For the first time: something crosses his face that is not calculation.

The camera holds on it. Long.

Then he looks down at the burn scar on his palm. He closes his fist.

**SMASH CUT TO BLACK.**

# EPISODE 9: मुकाम (MUKAAM — RECKONING)
**The Frozen Lake Meeting | The Daughter's Choice | Vriddhak Alone**

**KEY SCENE — THE FROZEN LAKE:**
Arjak and Rudraveg meet on a frozen lake between territories. No armies. The conversation is shot in a single, slow two-shot that lasts four minutes without cutting — the longest take in the series. As they speak, the camera VERY slowly closes the distance between them, beginning at their backs to each other and ending with them facing each other in close-up. They do not move. The camera moves for them.

**KEY SCENE — DHRUPAD'S DAUGHTER:**
She is freed. She is wearing Ashvaghor colors. She looks at her father, and he at her.

**DHRUPAD'S DAUGHTER:**
*"Main ghar aana chahti toh tum nahin aate."*
("If I had wanted to come home, you would not have needed to come.")

She returns to the Ashvaghor camp. The camera holds on Dhrupad. Just his face. For a long time. The audience watches a man understand that the price has been paid and nothing was bought.

**KEY SCENE — VRIDDHAK:**
In the ice-temple. Alone. His hand on the Trishul-shield.

*"Ek baccha bacha tha. Main jaanta tha yeh din aayega."*
("One child was left. I knew this day would come.")

Not afraid. Not regretful. A man who has completed what he set out to do, regardless of what it cost.

# EPISODE 10: हिमंत (THE END OF ICE)
**The Battle | Virangi's Choice | The Whisper Revealed | Season 2 Tease**

**THE BATTLE — THE IMAGE MADE REAL:**
The battlefield from the user's original image: fully realized. Both armies on the glacier. Torches. Black flags. Snow. Arjak on the Haimavat side. Rudraveg emerging from the Ashvaghor ranks.

They have agreed to nothing. The armies are still armies. The battle has begun. Thousands are fighting.

**VIRANGI'S CHOICE:**
She takes the Trishul-shield from the ice-temple. She carries it into the center of the battlefield. Alone. Unarmed except for herself.

She plants it in the ice where both armies can see it.

She stands beside it. She does not speak.

**THE WAR STOPS.**

Not because of strategy. Not because of tactics. Because the symbol that both sides were fighting over — one to protect, one to capture — is standing in open ground, held by one woman, and she is neither.

**THE FINAL CONFRONTATION — FROM EPISODE 1:**
Arjak and Rudraveg, face to face. This is the scene from the original image. We are finally here.

The full viral dialogue exchange.

And then — Arjak kneels. Deliberately. Lays his axe on the ice between them.

**RUDRAVEG leans down and whispers:**

*(NOW SUBTITLED — the words we were denied in Episode 1)*

**RUDRAVEG:**
*"Vaa. Ab ghar chaltey hain."*
("Come. Now we go home.")

**WHAT THIS MEANS NOW:**
Not victory. Not surrender. The end of a war that was never about territory. A man calling his nephew — the only family he has left — to come home to something that has to be built from what remains.

**ARJAK'S EXPRESSION:** Not defeat. Not relief. The expression of a man who just learned that home was never behind him. It is ahead. And it has to be made.

**SEASON 2 TEASE:**
The glacier groans. A child looks up. Emerging from the ancient ice: stone, carved, impossibly old. A Trishul — with a fourth prong.

# APPENDIX: THE THREE VISUAL LAWS IN PRACTICE

## LAW 1 — THE TRISHUL IN EACH EPISODE

| Episode | Where the Trishul appears that has not appeared before |
|---------|------------------------------------------------------|
| Ep 1 | On the shield, in torchlight — CAPTURED |
| Ep 2 | In a child's hands, placed in fire as offering — the flame burns silver |
| Ep 3 | As a miniature carved in bone — in Rudraveg's hands, not as weapon but as memory |
| Ep 4 | In the old stone tablet — as text, as information, as the reason for everything |
| Ep 5 | Etched into Arjak's axe haft — close, personal, a thing he carries without understanding |
| Ep 6 | On the black battle flags — as a trophy flown by the enemy |
| Ep 7 | Absent. The episode with no Trishul shot. Its absence is the presence of loss. |
| Ep 8 | In Vriddhak's burned palm — it has been inside his body all along |
| Ep 9 | Reflected in the frozen lake where Arjak and Rudraveg meet — in the ice, not above it |
| Ep 10 | Planted in the open battlefield — finally belonging to no one, finally free |

**Law: Episode 7 has NO Trishul shot.** It is the episode of Bhedak's death. The symbol is absent because something is absent that cannot be replaced.

## LAW 2 — THE BLUE EYE MATCH
Every episode where both characters appear, even in separate scenes, includes at least one shot where the light hits both of their eyes at the same temperature and color. The editor must find or create this match in post.

## LAW 3 — THE GLACIER CLOCK
| Episode | State of the glacier |
|---------|---------------------|
| Ep 1 | Full. The world as it was. |
| Ep 3 | First crack visible in the ice-temple wall |
| Ep 5 | A stream flowing through the glacier pass — water, for the first time |
| Ep 7 | The boulder Bhedak always used as a landmark has fallen — the glacier beneath it retreated |
| Ep 9 | The frozen lake where Arjak and Rudraveg meet has one cracked section — they stand away from it |
| Ep 10 | The glacier has retreated far enough that its base is visible — raw rock, ancient and bare. The world has changed. The ice age is ending. |
